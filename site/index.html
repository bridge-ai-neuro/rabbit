A foundation model for speech- and language-evoked brain activity that enables accurate zero-shot and few-shot prediction.

Foundation models like CLIP transformed AI by separating what is shared from what is task-specific: strong zero-shot prediction, then rapid adaptation from only a few examples. Brain encoding never had this. Every new participant required collecting fMRI and fitting a new model. RABBiT changes that. It is a foundation model for speech- and language-evoked fMRI that supports both zero-shot prediction and efficient few-shot personalization within the same architecture. From audio alone, it predicts the brain response shared across listeners, outperforming the state-of-the-art billion-parameter multimodal foundation model in language regions while remaining compact enough to run interactively in a browser. With only ~10 minutes of a person's own fMRI, it learns a tiny subject-specific correction that outperforms standard participant-specific methods using ~1000× fewer trainable parameters. We validated RABBiT on 324 unseen participants across 16 held-out studies and showed that it reproduces canonical findings from language neuroscience. Now it's public. Try it live, right here. :D

01 Two ways to use one model

How much fMRI do you have?

Both regimes below address the same challenge: a new listener hearing speech the model has never encountered. The only difference is how much of that listener's fMRI is available. One model can support both because speech-evoked brain responses contain two kinds of information: reliable structure shared across listeners, and individual differences that become increasingly important in higher-order language regions.

Zero-shot · no fMRI

New speech, no brain data

No fMRI from this person at all. Audio goes in (even speech the model never trained on), and the response shared across listeners comes out.

Predicts the shared brain response to new speech, for entirely new listeners
Recovers group-level auditory and language responses directly from audio
Few-shot · ~10 minutes

The same, plus a little of their fMRI

Same new listener, same novel speech, now with ~10 minutes of their own fMRI. RABBiT learns a compact subject-specific correction that captures what makes their brain unique.

No voxel-wise model to fit, only a small subject-specific correction
Improves exactly where brains differ most: higher-order language cortex
02 Result 1 · It behaves like a brain

It organizes like a brain.

Accuracy alone is not enough. If RABBiT is learning meaningful language representations rather than simply fitting data, it should reproduce established properties of the human language network. It does, both from its predictions and from the representations it learns internally.

A classic localizer

The language network, reproduced.

Present RABBiT with the standard intelligibility contrast (intact speech versus acoustically matched but unintelligible speech) and subtract the predicted responses. A left-lateralized fronto-temporo-parietal language network emerges: frontal, lateral temporal, inferior-parietal and angular regions.

Early auditory cortex, which responds similarly to both conditions, largely cancels, exactly as expected. RABBiT was never trained on this contrast or on any controlled localizer.

Emergent hierarchy

It rebuilds the processing stream.

Each brain region is represented by a learned query inside the model. Starting from primary auditory cortex, repeatedly moving to the most similar not-yet-visited region reconstructs the canonical progression from primary auditory cortex through belt regions, STG / STS, temporal association cortex, and finally parietal and frontal language regions.

The model is never given this ordering. It emerges purely from the learned representational geometry.

Together these make RABBiT more than a predictive model. It becomes a computational model organism for speech and language, an in-silico brain that can be localized, contrasted, and probed without collecting new fMRI. The next two sections ask how accurately it predicts brain responses: first without any additional fMRI, then with only ~10 minutes of participant-specific data.

03 Result 2 · Zero-shot: no fMRI from the person

The shared response, from speech alone.

The hardest setting: no fMRI from this listener, and speech the model has never encountered. From audio alone, RABBiT predicts the reliable group-level response shared across listeners. Below, we first show one held-out participant, then summarize performance across all 324 unseen participants.

Zero-shot accuracy

Validated across multiple novel studies and stimuli.

Without any retraining, RABBiT already predicts speech-evoked activity across auditory cortex and the temporal language network, the parts of the response that are reliably shared across people.

vs a billion-parameter model

Ahead of a far larger model.

Compared with TRIBEv2, a billion-parameter multimodal brain foundation model, RABBiT's largest gains concentrate in auditory and temporal language cortex, despite being trained on only six participants and predicting at four times the cortical resolution.

Region by region

The advantage, quantified.

Region by region, RABBiT's largest gains occur in auditory cortex, STG / STS, temporal pole, insula / FOp, and mPFC. The two models perform similarly across much of higher-order language cortex, with precuneus / PCC the one region where TRIBEv2 significantly leads.

Visual and motor regions remain near chance for both, as expected for an audio-only model.

vs the inter-subject consistency measure (ISC)

It reliably reaches or exceeds the ISC.

Across 324 unseen participants, RABBiT reaches, and on one cohort slightly exceeds, the leave-one-out inter-subject consistency estimate, indicating that its remaining error is approaching the reliability of the target itself. It matches a much larger per-subject readout while remaining substantially smaller, and both the billion-parameter TRIBEv2 and the linear baseline remain below this reference.

Gray dots show peer inter-subject consistency; the dashed line is the leave-one-out consistency estimate.

04 Result 3 · Few-shot: a few minutes of fMRI data

Ten minutes completes the picture.

Zero-shot predicts what listeners share. The parts that make a brain individual, primarily higher-order language regions, require only a small amount of participant-specific data. About 10 minutes is enough for RABBiT to learn that remaining structure.

Few-shot accuracy

Sharper across the language network.

A compact subject-specific correction, learned from ~10 minutes of listening, substantially improves prediction across the language network. The model itself does not change; only the small personalization pathway is updated.

Where ten minutes lands

The gain lands on the language network.

Comparing few-shot to zero-shot directly shows that almost all of the improvement occurs in higher-order language cortex (frontal, inferior-parietal, medial frontal, and precuneus / PCC regions), while auditory cortex, already well captured zero-shot, changes very little.

Every region improves

The gains are exactly where brains differ.

Across the regions that vary most across individuals, the 10-minute correction significantly improves prediction throughout the language network. Both bars correspond to the same RABBiT model: before and after personalization.

vs per-voxel ridge

Beats ridge with a far lower budget.

RABBiT improves over its own zero-shot prediction after only 5 minutes of calibration and consistently outperforms both voxel-wise ridge baselines through 40 minutes. It achieves this while updating only ~115K parameters per participant, roughly 1000× fewer than voxel-wise ridge.

At ten minutes

Up to ~90% better, region by region.

Relative to its own zero-shot prediction, ~10 minutes increases performance by roughly 30 to 90% in IFG, supramarginal gyrus, angular gyrus, MFG / DLPFC, mPFC, and precuneus / PCC, the very regions identified by the model as most individual across listeners.

05 How it works

Inside the model.

Two ideas make both transfer regimes possible. One learns how different brain regions selectively integrate information from speech. The other separates what listeners share from what makes each brain unique, allowing the same model to support both zero-shot and few-shot prediction. Then two checks show which components are load-bearing, and why a few minutes of data help exactly where they do.

TBT Temporal Brain Transformer

Region-specific attention over the speech stream.

Rather than fitting an independent readout for every voxel, RABBiT learns one query per cortical region. Each query attends across the speech sequence and decides which moments of audio matter for that region, producing a compact per-region representation $z_i$. 30 regions (15 bilateral HCP-MMP1 groupings on fsaverage6) replace millions of independent per-voxel weights.

$$\alpha_{i,t} = \operatorname{softmax}_t \! \left( \langle W_Q\, q_i, \; W_K\, x_t \rangle \right), \qquad z_i = \sum_t \alpha_{i,t} \cdot W_V\, x_t \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{d_o}$$
TBT · the readout

One query per region.

Each brain region learns its own query over the speech stream. Rather than pooling speech uniformly, every region selectively gathers the moments most relevant to it, producing a compact region-level representation zi.

SID Shared–Idiosyncratic Decomposition

What everyone shares + what makes a brain individual.

Each region's response is written as a shared population subspace ($\Phi_i$) plus a small per-person deviation ($\Delta_{i,s}$). The shared part is what drives the zero-shot prediction for anyone; the deviation is the small, structured difference that the few-shot step tunes for an individual. This split is exactly why one model covers both regimes, and why ~10 minutes of data is enough to adapt.

$$\hat{y}^{(s)}_i = \pi_i(z_i) \, \Phi_i \;+\; \rho_i(z_i) \, \Delta_{i,s}$$

$\Phi_i$ is the shared basis (the population response subspace for region $i$); $\Delta_{i,s}$ is the per-person deviation basis; $\pi_i, \rho_i$ are small heads that read out from the region representation $z_i$. Few-shot updates only $\rho_i$ (the deviation coefficients), and freezes everything else.

End to end

From speech to brain.

A brain-tuned speech model converts audio into speech representations. The Temporal Brain Transformer routes them into region-level representations. SID then separates every prediction into a shared population response and a compact participant-specific deviation before producing high-resolution fMRI predictions.

The entire model is trained end-to-end on six participants from CNeuroMod Friends.

Why it works: what each piece buys you

Ablate a component, or leave a person out, and watch zero-shot accuracy move.

Ablations

Which pieces matter.

Brain-tuning contributes the largest improvement in zero-shot prediction, while removing the Temporal Brain Transformer also significantly reduces performance. SID matches a full participant-specific readout using roughly five times fewer parameters, and replacing wav2vec2 with a much larger speech model provides little additional benefit.

The diagnostic

Why minutes help where they do.

Leaving one listener out of the population average barely changes early auditory cortex: the response there is largely shared, so zero-shot already predicts it well. Higher-order language regions change dramatically, indicating that they contain the strongest participant-specific structure.

Those are precisely the regions where few-shot adaptation produces its largest gains.
